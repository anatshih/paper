MEDIATION ANALYSIS REPORT

Stage 9: The Mediating Role of Job Satisfaction in the PsyCap–Work Engagement Relationship

Study Title:

The Mediating Role of Job Satisfaction in the Relationship Between

Psychological Capital and Work Engagement Among Academic Staff

at Al-Quds Open University, Palestine

Sample Size: N = 270 | Bootstrap Samples: 5,000 | CI Level: 95%

Method: PROCESS Macro v5.0 (Hayes, 2022) — Model 4 | IBM SPSS Statistics v26

Date: May 2026

1. Introduction and Purpose

The final analytical stage of this study tests the core theoretical proposition that Job Satisfaction mediates the relationship between Psychological Capital (PsyCap) and Work Engagement among academic staff at Al-Quds Open University (QOU), Palestine. This hypothesis — designated H4 — represents the study's principal contribution to the literature: it posits that PsyCap does not operate in isolation as a direct driver of engagement, but rather works substantially through its capacity to enhance faculty members' satisfaction with their professional roles, which in turn energises and sustains their work engagement.

The four hypotheses examined across Stages 7–9 of this study are:

H1: PsyCap has a significant positive effect on Work Engagement.

H2: PsyCap has a significant positive effect on Job Satisfaction.

H3: Job Satisfaction has a significant positive effect on Work Engagement.

H4: Job Satisfaction mediates the relationship between PsyCap and Work Engagement.

H1–H3 were confirmed through Pearson correlation analysis (Stage 7) and simple linear regression (Stage 8). Stage 9 now formally tests H4 using the PROCESS Macro for SPSS (Version 5.0) by Andrew F. Hayes (2022), which implements a bootstrap-based approach to indirect effect estimation — the current methodological standard for mediation testing in social science research (Preacher & Hayes, 2008; Shrout & Bolger, 2002).

2. Analytical Method: PROCESS Macro Model 4

Mediation analysis was conducted using PROCESS Macro Version 5.0, Model 4 (Hayes, 2022), implemented within IBM SPSS Statistics. Model 4 is the standard simple mediation model, which simultaneously estimates: (a) the effect of the independent variable (PsyCap) on the mediator (Job Satisfaction); (b) the joint effect of both PsyCap and Job Satisfaction on the dependent variable (Work Engagement); (c) the total effect of PsyCap on Work Engagement; (d) the direct effect of PsyCap on Work Engagement after controlling for Job Satisfaction; and (e) the indirect effect of PsyCap on Work Engagement through Job Satisfaction.

The indirect effect was estimated using bias-corrected bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples and 95% confidence intervals. The bootstrap method is strongly preferred over older approaches (e.g., Sobel test) because it does not assume normality of the sampling distribution of the indirect effect — an assumption that is frequently violated in practice — and has been shown to have superior statistical power for detecting true mediation effects (Preacher & Hayes, 2008). The indirect effect is considered statistically significant if its 95% bootstrap confidence interval (BootCI) does not contain zero (Hayes, 2022).

The mediation model tested in this study is structured as follows:

X (Independent Variable): Psychological Capital — composite mean of PCQ-24 (24 items; α = .939)

M (Mediator): Job Satisfaction — composite mean of MSQ-20 (20 items; α = .939)

Y (Dependent Variable): Work Engagement — composite mean of UWES-9 (9 items; α = .890)

The type of mediation is determined by examining whether the direct effect (c') remains statistically significant after controlling for the mediator. If c' is significant and reduced (but not zero), partial mediation is indicated; if c' becomes non-significant, full mediation is indicated (Hayes, 2022). In either case, the presence of a significant indirect effect via bootstrapping is the definitive criterion for confirming mediation (Preacher & Hayes, 2008).

3.2 Path a: PsyCap → Job Satisfaction

The 'a' path — representing the effect of PsyCap on the mediator, Job Satisfaction — was statistically significant (B = .407, SE = .045, t(268) = 9.078, p < .001, 95% CI [.319, .495]). This indicates that faculty members at QOU with higher levels of psychological capital report significantly greater overall job satisfaction. The path estimate of B = .407 indicates that for every one-unit increase in PsyCap, Job Satisfaction is predicted to increase by 0.407 units, with this relationship explaining 23.5% of the variance in Job Satisfaction (R² = .235). This finding satisfies the first precondition for mediation: the independent variable must significantly predict the mediator.

3.3 Path b: Job Satisfaction → Work Engagement

The 'b' path — representing the effect of Job Satisfaction on Work Engagement while simultaneously controlling for PsyCap — was highly significant (B = .675, SE = .066, t(267) = 10.226, p < .001, 95% CI [.545, .805]). This indicates that, even after accounting for PsyCap, Job Satisfaction remains a powerful predictor of Work Engagement in the QOU sample. The path b estimate (B = .675) is notably large, underscoring Job Satisfaction's role as the proximal mechanism through which PsyCap's influence on engagement is channelled. This satisfies the second precondition for mediation: the mediator must significantly predict the outcome after controlling for the independent variable.

3.4 Total Effect (Path c): PsyCap → Work Engagement

The total effect (path c) of PsyCap on Work Engagement — representing the unmediated, bivariate relationship — was statistically significant (B = .587, SE = .057, t(268) = 10.301, p < .001, 95% CI [.475, .699]). This is consistent with the regression finding in Stage 8 (Model 1: R² = .284, β = .533, p < .001). The total effect represents the sum of the direct effect and the indirect effect: c = c' + (a × b) = .313 + .274 = .587, which is internally consistent with the PROCESS output.

3.5 Direct Effect (Path c'): PsyCap → Work Engagement Controlling Job Satisfaction

After introducing Job Satisfaction as a mediator, the direct effect of PsyCap on Work Engagement (path c') was B = .313, SE = .055, t(267) = 5.649, p < .001, 95% CI [.204, .422]. Critically, this direct effect remained statistically significant after controlling for Job Satisfaction, though it was substantially reduced from the total effect of B = .587. This reduction — from B = .587 (path c) to B = .313 (path c') — represents the portion of PsyCap's effect on Work Engagement that is explained by the mediating pathway through Job Satisfaction.

This increment in explained variance (ΔR² = .485 − .284 = .201) attributable to adding Job Satisfaction into the model confirms its significant incremental contribution and further supports its role as a meaningful mediating variable.

The 95% bootstrap confidence interval for the indirect effect [.199, .360] does not contain zero, which is the definitive criterion for statistical significance in bootstrap-based mediation testing (Hayes, 2022; Preacher & Hayes, 2008). This confirms that Job Satisfaction significantly mediates the relationship between Psychological Capital and Work Engagement among QOU academic staff — providing formal statistical support for H4.

The magnitude of the indirect effect (B = .274) is substantively meaningful. It indicates that approximately 46.7% of PsyCap's total effect on Work Engagement (indirect effect / total effect = .274 / .587 = .467) is channelled through the mediating pathway of Job Satisfaction. This proportion is large enough to designate Job Satisfaction as a important mediating mechanism in the PsyCap–engagement relationship, while the significant direct effect (B = .313, p < .001) confirms that PsyCap also exerts a meaningful direct influence on engagement that is independent of its effect on satisfaction.

4. Type of Mediation: Partial Mediation

The pattern of results — a significant indirect effect (B = .274, BootCI [.199, .360]) accompanied by a significant but reduced direct effect (c': B = .313, p < .001) — is consistent with partial mediation (Hayes, 2022). In partial mediation, the mediator explains a meaningful portion of the independent variable's effect on the outcome, but does not completely account for the relationship — leaving a significant residual direct pathway from PsyCap to Work Engagement.

The partial mediation finding is theoretically appropriate and meaningful for the QOU context. It indicates two complementary mechanisms through which PsyCap influences work engagement among Palestinian academic faculty:

The mediated pathway (46.7%): higher PsyCap predicts higher Job Satisfaction, and this elevated satisfaction in turn energises and sustains Work Engagement. This pathway reflects the sequential process described by Conservation of Resources (COR) theory (Hobfoll, 1989): psychological resources (PsyCap) enable more positive appraisals of work conditions and relationships (satisfaction), which then mobilise the energetic investment characteristic of engagement. In the QOU context, where faculty must navigate significant institutional constraints, PsyCap appears to function partly by enabling faculty to extract greater satisfaction from their work — buffering against structural dissatisfiers such as pay and advancement limitations — and this elevated satisfaction then is associated with higher engagement.

The direct pathway (53.3%): PsyCap also shows a direct, satisfaction-independent positive association with Work Engagement. This direct pathway reflects the motivational properties of PsyCap itself: faculty members who are high in self-efficacy, hope, resilience, and optimism are energised, goal-directed, and positively oriented in ways that directly sustain their vigor, dedication, and absorption in academic work — without necessarily requiring satisfaction as an intermediary step. This is consistent with the Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) model (Bakker & Demerouti, 2008), which identifies positive psychological resources as direct personal resources that fuel engagement independently of job satisfaction.

6. Contextual Interpretation and Research Implications

The mediation findings carry significant theoretical and practical implications for higher education management in Palestine, and for the broader study of PsyCap in non-Western, resource-constrained organisational contexts.

6.1 Theoretical Contributions

This study provides empirical evidence from a Palestinian higher education context that Job Satisfaction partially mediates the PsyCap–Work Engagement relationship — a finding that contributes to the growing body of literature extending PsyCap research beyond Western organisational settings (Luthans et al., 2007; Avey et al., 2011). The mediation ratio of 46.7% is consistent with the partial mediation results reported in comparable studies from developing and non-Western contexts (e.g., Wardani, 2019; Mehdad, 2019), suggesting that this mediating pathway may be a cross-culturally robust mechanism.

The finding that 48.5% of variance in Work Engagement is explained by the combined model (PsyCap + Job Satisfaction) — compared to 28.4% by PsyCap alone — demonstrates the theoretical value of incorporating Job Satisfaction as a mediating variable. Without accounting for the satisfaction pathway, a substantial portion of the mechanism linking PsyCap to engagement remains unexplained. This study therefore contributes a more complete and nuanced model of the determinants of faculty work engagement in Palestinian universities.

6.2 Practical Implications for Al-Quds Open University

The partial mediation result has preliminary practical implications for institutional strategy at QOU. It suggests that two complementary intervention pathways are available to enhance faculty work engagement:

Pathway 1 — Develop PsyCap directly: Investing in faculty psychological capital development — through self-efficacy workshops, coaching programmes, resilience training, and optimism cultivation — can enhance work engagement both directly and indirectly. The direct pathway (B = .313) confirms that PsyCap development yields engagement benefits independent of its effect on satisfaction, making it a robust and versatile intervention target. QOU may consider structured PsyCap development programmes modelled on the PsyCap Intervention (PCI) approach documented in the international literature (Luthans et al., 2007).

Pathway 2 — Enhance Job Satisfaction: The mediated pathway (46.7% of total effect) confirms that improvements in Job Satisfaction represent an important lever for enhancing work engagement indirectly. Given the item-level finding from Stage 5 that pay satisfaction (M = 3.14) is the lowest-scoring MSQ facet, addressing compensation constraints may yield significant returns. However, the EFA findings (Stage 6) suggest that intrinsic satisfaction dimensions — particularly professional autonomy, sense of accomplishment, collegial relationships, and community recognition — also drive the second satisfaction factor, and these dimensions may be more amenable to institutional intervention in the short term.

The geographic dispersion of QOU's academic staff across 15 branches in the West Bank adds an important contextual dimension to these recommendations. Strategies for PsyCap development and satisfaction improvement will need to be adapted for delivery across diverse branch environments — potentially through blended learning platforms, branch-level peer support networks, and decentralised academic leadership structures that empower faculty at local levels.
