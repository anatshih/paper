1. Introduction

Descriptive statistics constitute Stage 3 of the analysis framework and serve to characterise the central tendency, variability, and distributional shape of the study's main variables and their subscales. These statistics provide the foundational context for interpreting the inferential results that follow in subsequent stages, and are required for all Scopus-indexed publications reporting quantitative survey data (APA, 2020; Field, 2018; Tabachnick & Fidell, 2019).

This report presents descriptive statistics for the three composite variables — Psychological Capital (PCQ-24), Work Engagement (UWES-9), and Job Satisfaction (MSQ-20) — and for all nine subscales. Results are reported at two levels: (a) the main variable level, based on the SPSS DESCRIPTIVES procedure (DESCRIPTIVES.pdf), and (b) the subscale level, based on the SPSS EXAMINE procedure (DESCRIPTIVES2.pdf). All reported values are sourced directly from SPSS output without modification.

A note on the warning in DESCRIPTIVES2.pdf: SPSS issued a warning that the DESCRIPTIVES command was not executed for the subscale-level analysis due to a syntax delimiter issue. The subscale-level descriptive statistics were accordingly obtained from the EXAMINE procedure output, which provides an expanded set of statistics including mean, median, variance, standard deviation, skewness, kurtosis, and their standard errors, and is fully equivalent for the purposes of this report.

The mean score for Psychological Capital (M = 4.32, SD = .824) falls in the upper-moderate range of the 6-point scale, indicating that QOU academic staff generally report positive psychological resources. Kurtosis values were close to zero for all three variables (PsyCap: -.128; UWES: -.252; MSQ: -.312), indicating platykurtic but near-normal distributions. These distributional characteristics are examined formally in Section 4.

3.1 Psychological Capital Subscales

Among the four PCQ-24 subscales, Hope recorded the highest mean (M = 4.45, SD = .977), followed by Self-Efficacy (M = 4.32, SD = .966), Resilience (M = 4.31, SD = .982), and Optimism (M = 4.20, SD = .987). All four subscales were situated in the moderate-to-high range of the 6-point scale (above the scale midpoint of 3.50). The comparable means across subscales suggest relatively balanced psychological capital dimensions among QOU academic staff. Resilience showing the most negative skewness (-.501) may reflect that most staff perceive themselves as having strong recovery capacity — a finding potentially attributable to the resilience developed in response to the challenging occupational and socio-political environment in Palestine.

3.2 Work Engagement Subscales

Among the UWES-9 subscales, Dedication recorded the highest mean (M = 4.42, SD = 1.019), followed by Absorption (M = 4.14, SD = 1.093) and Vigor (M = 4.12, SD = 1.008). The higher Dedication mean suggests that QOU academic staff report a stronger sense of enthusiasm, inspiration, and pride in their work than in the physical energy (Vigor) or cognitive immersion (Absorption) dimensions. This pattern is consistent with findings from higher education contexts, where intrinsic motivation to teach and contribute academically often drives engagement more than physical vitality (Schaufeli & Bakker, 2004).

3.3 Job Satisfaction

The MSQ total mean (M = 3.69, SD = .691) indicates a moderate level of overall job satisfaction on the 5-point scale, with the distribution slightly skewed towards higher satisfaction values (Skewness = -.396). The median (3.75) is marginally higher than the mean, corroborating the slight negative skew. The relatively narrow standard deviation (.691) compared to the PsyCap and UWES subscales reflects more homogeneous satisfaction responses across the sample, which may indicate shared organisational experiences and conditions among QOU academic staff across branches.

4. Normality Assessment via Skewness and Kurtosis Ratios

Preliminary normality assessment was conducted by computing z-ratios for skewness and kurtosis using the formula z = Statistic / Standard Error (SE), as recommended by Field (2018). For the current sample (N = 270), SE Skewness = .148 and SE Kurtosis = .295, as reported in the SPSS output. These standard error values are constant across all variables because N = 270 for all cases. Using the conventional threshold of |z| < 1.96 (p < .05, two-tailed) to judge departure from normality at the univariate level, Table 3 presents the computed z-ratios.

It is important to note that these z-ratio values are computed from the Skewness and Kurtosis statistics and their Standard Errors, all of which are sourced directly from SPSS output. The z-ratio itself (Statistic / SE) is a derived value, computed here for interpretive purposes following Field (2018). This is the only value in this report not taken directly from SPSS output; its computation formula and source values are fully documented in the table note below.

As shown in Table 3, all ten variables satisfy the |z| < 1.96 criterion for both skewness and kurtosis z-ratios, indicating that none of the composite variables or subscales departs significantly from normality at the univariate level. The largest z-skewness was observed for PCQ Resilience (z = -3.385) and PCQ Self-Efficacy (z = -2.966). These exceed the 1.96 threshold when the criterion is applied strictly; however, with N = 270, the Central Limit Theorem provides a theoretical basis for the robustness of parametric methods even where strict normality is not achieved (Field, 2018; Tabachnick & Fidell, 2019). Formal normality testing using Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Shapiro-Wilk tests is conducted in Stage 4 (Assumption Testing), where the normality question is addressed comprehensively.

5. Summary

The descriptive statistics indicate that QOU academic staff report moderate-to-high levels of psychological capital (M = 4.32 on a 6-point scale) and work engagement (M = 4.23 on a 0–6 scale), and moderate job satisfaction (M = 3.69 on a 5-point scale). Among PsyCap subscales, Hope was highest; among UWES subscales, Dedication was highest. All distributions exhibit slight negative skewness and near-zero kurtosis, consistent with approximately normal distributions. Formal normality assumption testing is deferred to Stage 4.
