1. Introduction

Data cleaning and screening represent the foundational step of any quantitative research process. Conducting these procedures prior to substantive analysis ensures that the data are accurate, complete, and free from systematic errors that could bias subsequent results (Hair et al., 2019; Tabachnick & Fidell, 2019). This report documents all cleaning and screening procedures applied to the dataset collected from 270 academic staff members at Al-Quds Open University (QOU), Palestine.

The dataset comprises responses to three standardised instruments: the Psychological Capital Questionnaire (PCQ-24), the Utrecht Work Engagement Scale (UWES-9), and the Minnesota Satisfaction Questionnaire (MSQ-20), along with six demographic variables. All analyses reported herein were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics, and all values are reported directly from SPSS output without modification.

2. Missing Value Analysis

Missing value analysis was performed using the SPSS Frequencies procedure (Analyze → Descriptive Statistics → Frequencies), specifying all 53 measurement items and six demographic variables. The output confirmed that N Valid = 270 and N Missing = 0 across every variable in the dataset, as verified from the Statistics tables in the SPSS output.

This result is attributable to the use of a structured electronic questionnaire with mandatory response fields, which effectively prevented item non-response. Accordingly, no imputation or case-deletion procedures were required.

3. Data Range Verification

To verify that all recorded responses fell within theoretically permissible scale boundaries, the minimum and maximum observed values for each item were extracted from the SPSS Descriptive Statistics output (Analyze → Descriptive Statistics → Descriptives, requesting Minimum and Maximum). Results are presented in Table 2 below.

All 24 PCQ-24 items recorded a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 6, consistent with the instrument's 6-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 6 = Strongly Agree). All 9 UWES-9 items recorded values within the 0–6 range, consistent with the instrument's scoring convention that permits a minimum score of 0 (Never). All 20 MSQ-20 items recorded values within the 1–5 range. No out-of-range values were detected across any item, confirming the integrity of data entry and coding.

4. Outlier Detection

Outlier screening was conducted at the respondent level using the variable Respondent_SD, which was computed by SPSS as the standard deviation of each respondent's responses across all 53 items. This approach identifies cases exhibiting abnormally low within-person variance — a reliable indicator of straight-line or careless responding (Meade & Craig, 2012). The Respondent_SD frequency table was generated via Analyze → Descriptive Statistics → Frequencies.

The observed Respondent_SD values ranged from a minimum of 0.81 to a maximum of 1.77. The commonly applied threshold for flagging straight-line respondents is SD < 0.50 (DeSimone et al., 2015). Since the minimum observed SD was 0.81, no respondents were flagged for removal on this criterion. No respondent exhibited an exceptionally high SD suggestive of random responding. All 270 cases were therefore retained.

In addition, z-scores were computed for all 53 items (variables Zpcq1–Zmsq20) via the SPSS Descriptives procedure (Save standardized values as variables option). Item-level z-score distributions were inspected and no item-level extreme outliers (|z| > 3.29) were identified that would necessitate case removal.

5. Reverse-Item Identification and Coding Verification

Inspection of the PCQ-24 frequency distributions revealed three negatively worded items that require reverse scoring prior to subscale computation. These items are embedded within the Resilience and Optimism subscales and are marked with (R) in the original instrument (Luthans et al., 2007). Their identification was confirmed by comparing their observed means against positively worded items within the same subscale: the three reverse items recorded means below 3.00, whereas positively worded items consistently recorded means above 4.00, indicating that reverse scoring had not yet been applied in the raw dataset.

The reverse-scoring formula for a 6-point scale is: Recoded value = 7 − Original value. This recoding must be applied to pcq13, pcq20, and pcq23 before computing subscale means, composite scores, reliability coefficients, or any subsequent analysis.

7. Conclusion

Data cleaning and screening confirm that the dataset meets all quality requirements for quantitative analysis. The complete sample of N = 270 is retained without deletion. Zero missing values, zero out-of-range responses, and zero outliers were detected. The only pending preparatory action is the reverse scoring of pcq13, pcq20, and pcq23, which must be executed in SPSS (Transform → Recode into Different Variables, using the formula: Recoded = 7 − Original) prior to any reliability, validity, or inferential analysis.

The analysis will proceed to Stage 2: Demographic Analysis.
